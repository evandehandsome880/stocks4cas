About the project

Why this site exists

Six common trading rules, one shared simulated market, and every number published so you can check it yourself.

The work

A price generator, six rules written down before they were tested, and this site. It rebuilds from one command and publishes its own logs.

The method

Every rule runs on the same prices, so the differences in the results come from the rules rather than from the data. The seed is fixed, so the run repeats.

The limits

The market is simulated and the costs of real trading are missing. Nothing here predicts a price, and none of it is advice.

The CAS project behind this site

IBDP Creativity and Service, 5 January to 31 August 2026

stocks4cas is one part of an IBDP CAS project. The CAS project runs from 5 January to 31 August 2026, and the simulated market it studies covers 1 March to 1 September 2026. Alongside this site the project covers AI-assisted research, workflow automation, and short videos written for a student audience.

Real, purposeful activity with significant outcomes

The work produced something that runs: a price generator, six tested rules, a published trade log, and a site that rebuilds from one command.

Personal challenge

None of us had written a backtest, a public website or an automation workflow before, and the statistics behind the rules were new to all four of us.

Thoughtful consideration: planning, reviewing progress, reporting

We planned the pipeline, reviewed the results as they came in, and reported them here and on the news page, including the strategies that lost money.

Reflection on outcomes and personal learning

The limits chapter records what we got wrong: the trading costs we left out, the single seed, and a price model that behaves more politely than a real market.

Methodology & data

How the simulation was generated

Window: 1 March to 1 September 2026, which is 132 trading days.

Capital: every strategy starts with $1,000,000 of simulated cash.

Prices: geometric Brownian motion with a drift and volatility set per ticker, seeded so the run repeats.

Metrics: net P&L, total return, annualised Sharpe ratio and maximum drawdown.

Engine: Python with NumPy and Pandas (simulate.py), written out as JSON and bundled for this site.

Teaching paths: the three shapes on the prerequisites page and the five game rounds come from the same engine, under a second seed of their own.
