Three ideas, then six rules

No finance background needed. Read the three ideas and look at the chart, then meet the six strategies in one line each.

A price is the last trade

Someone paid that much for one share, minutes ago. It records what they thought the share was worth, not what the company is worth.

A rule you write first

You decide the rule before you know the outcome. That is what makes it testable, and it is the only thing separating a strategy from an opinion.

Risk is the size of the fall

A rule that gains 10% a year while dropping 40% on the way is much harder to hold than the yearly number suggests.

Three shapes, one market

Sixty days each, all starting at 100, generated by simulate.py
Loading the paths.

Climbing

The price rises for weeks with the odd bad day. A rule that buys whatever is already rising looks clever here.

Choppy

The price wanders and finishes near where it started. Rules that chase the last move pay for every entry and get little back.

Snap back

Every push away from the middle gets pulled back toward it, which is what a mean reversion rule bets on.

You only know which shape you were in once it is over. Choosing the rule after seeing the chart is the mistake this site is about.

The six strategies in plain words

Each rule needs the market to behave in a certain way

Buy and hold

Buy once and wait. Needs a market that rises over years rather than weeks.

Momentum

Buy what has already risen the most. Needs a trend that lasts long enough to pay for the entry.

Mean reversion

Buy what has just fallen hard, sell once it recovers. Needs prices that swing around a level instead of trending.

Moving average crossover

Buy when the short average rises above the long one, sell when it crosses back. Needs slow turns in one direction.

Dollar-cost averaging

Invest the same amount on a fixed schedule. Needs nothing except patience, which is the whole point.

Pairs trading

Two similar shares drift apart, so bet that they rejoin. Needs the pair to really be similar, and that is the hard part.

Five words you will see

Enough to read the other pages

Return

How much the money gained or lost, as a percentage of what you started with.

Volatility

How much the price jumps around. More volatility means wider swings in both directions.

Drawdown

The worst fall from a peak down to the low that followed it.

Sharpe ratio

Return divided by the size of the swings. Roughly, how much bumpiness each percent of return cost.

Backtest

Running a rule over past or simulated prices to see what it would have done.

And the catch

A backtest describes one possible past. It says nothing about tomorrow, and a rule adjusted until it looks good has usually been adjusted to that past.
